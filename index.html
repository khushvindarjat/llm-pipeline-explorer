Text to Space

A hands-on visual guide to how language models turn everyday words into numbers and map their meaning in 3D space.

How do computers understand sentences? They can't read like we do. Instead, they do two things: first, they chop sentences into small puzzle pieces called tokens. Second, they convert each sentence into a list of numbers (a vector) so it has a physical address in space. Sentences about similar topics land near each other. Let's see it in action:

1. Teaching the Computer Words

Give the computer some writing to read. It will count which words appear most often and build a vocabulary dictionary.

2. Plotting Sentences in 3D Space

Each sentence is turned into a 64-dimensional vector and flattened into 3D coordinates using Principal Component Analysis (PCA). Notice how sentences about the same topic naturally form little neighborhoods!

Click and drag the 3D plot to orbit around!

3. Add Your Own Sentence to the Map

Type anything—a sentence about food, nature, coffee, or walking—and watch it light up as a Terracotta Diamond right inside the 3D neighborhood.
